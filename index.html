    Letter from the author

    On cities, data, and who they serve

    I'm a PhD student at KAIST, working at the intersection of computational social science and urban studies. My research asks how cities produce and reproduce inequality — and how emerging AI systems, from mobility models to generative urban simulations, are beginning to reshape that process.

    Methods that make cities more legible — and more accountable.

    I combine large-scale administrative and mobile data, spatial statistics, and machine learning to study neighborhood change, uneven regional growth, and the socio-technical systems now embedded in urban governance. I'm broadly interested in building methods that make cities more legible — and more accountable — to the people who live in them.

    01

    Computational Social Science

    Society, at population scale

    Using large-scale behavioral, textual, and administrative data with statistical and machine-learning methods to study social structure at population scale.

    02

    Urban Inequality

    Who gets the city?

    Measuring how residential segregation, mobility access, and neighborhood opportunity are distributed — and how those patterns persist or shift over time.

    03

    Urban AI

    When algorithms plan

    Examining how AI systems — from predictive policing to generative urban design tools — are embedded into planning and governance, and with what consequences.

    04

    Regional Development

    Uneven ground

    Tracing uneven growth across regions and cities, and how infrastructure, policy, and agglomeration shape long-run spatial disparities.
